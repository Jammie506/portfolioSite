  3. 2017 - 2023

    Sports & Fitness Markievicz - Dublin City Council

    Where Every Learner Meets the Water at Their Own Level

    During my time with Dublin City Council, I managed and delivered swimming lessons to individuals across a wide range of ages and backgrounds. Towards the end of this role, I also qualified as a Fitness Instructor, adding group fitness classes and personal training to my responsibilities.

    This role was rich in practical learning that I continue to draw on today. Teaching adult group swimming lessons showed me that adults engage far more readily when there is an element of fun involved, introducing games typically used with younger learners helped many adults find their confidence in the water. Teaching individual lessons and fitness classes reinforced the importance of meeting learners where they are, rather than where we might expect them to be which is an awareness I have carried directly into classroom-based teaching.

    Managing children's group lessons deepened my understanding of team communication: aligning on expectations, both for the team and the learners, and actively supporting colleagues to bring their best. Above all, this role cemented for me the power of play in learning. Some children take to water naturally; others need a fun, safe entry point. I have seen the same pattern in every learning environment since, when something feels intimidating, learners close off, but when it is presented with warmth and playfulness, they open up.

  4. 2011 - 2017

    During secondary school, I volunteered with St. John's Ambulance Cadets, beginning with weekly meetings in First Aid and Nursing before progressing to Cadet Sergeant. In this role, I took on responsibility for teaching peers basic first aid and lifesaving skills.

Planning an Inclusive & Self-Reliant Course

Curriculum Design Inclusive Practice

A core part of this redesign was creating a learning experience that helps learners build the digital skills they need — not just for IT Skills, but for every subject in a digital‑first centre. As I worked through the process, a few realities shaped my approach.

This plan was shaped by a few key realities of the learning environment:

  • Learners rely on their laptops across all subjects, so building digital confidence early has a huge impact on their overall success.

  • IT Skills is technically a theory module, but it only comes alive through practical, hands‑on work. That means the sequence, pacing, and scaffolding need to be crystal clear.

  • Mixed‑ability groups need materials that support independence while still offering enough structure for those who are brand new to computers.

By grounding the redesign in UDL principles, I created a structure that lets learners move at their own pace, revisit skills whenever they need to, and feel more in control of their learning. The impact has been noticeable: learners are more confident using digital tools across all their modules, and the clearer progression has helped them stay focused, independent, and able to see tasks through to completion.
